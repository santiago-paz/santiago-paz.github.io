### Design specificity verdict

**Review.** This page could be anyone's portfolio. Change the name and the project titles, and it could be selling a designer or a consultant.

It matches two of the AI-default looks the owner's rules ban:
- **Look 1:** cream paper (#f5f4ed), a serif display with italic accent words, and one muted accent. The accent is forest green, not terracotta. A different color doesn't make it a different look.
- **Look 3:** hairline rules on every row, numbered caps labels like "01 / Selected work", and 3-4px corners.

It uses the same stock moves many template portfolios use:
- The surname is in green italics with a period.
- Every heading is a pun with an italic ending.
- A green dot signals "available".
- Abstract drawings sit where the products should be.
- The sign-off reads like a freelancer's.

Nothing in the look comes from the edge. There are three live products with real screens. The Trading Desk's retro desktop alone would be more memorable than all three drawings.

### Overall impression

The page is calm, careful and accessible. But it shows off a style instead of proving what was built. The edge is whole products built alone. The page never says that, and it never links to a live demo.

The biggest opportunity is to make the products the design. Real screens and "built alone" proof would fix the positioning and the template look at the same time.

### What's working

1. **The accessibility work holds up.** The skip link comes first. All 24 tab stops show a clear focus ring, in reading order. Resting text measures 4.66:1 or better. Nothing overflows at 320px. Reduced motion turns off every transition.
2. **The copy supports the positioning.** The tagline matches PRODUCT.md word for word and is the second-biggest line in the hero. Two card summaries lead with numbers: "12 LLM analyst agents" and "26 runnable labs". DiffCV admits its AI engine is unfinished.
3. **The work index scans well.** Three cards follow the CV order, then four compact rows. Seven projects are easy to take in, with no filters.

### Priority issues

**1. [P1] The edge never shows on the home page**
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md names one claim to protect: live products built alone. The page never says solo, alone or end-to-end. It has no link to trycontractlens.com, trading-desk.santiagopaz.com or reema.ar. A hiring manager can't check the claim from here.
- **Fix:**
  - Show each project's role line on its card. The lines are already in data/projects.json.
  - Add "Live demo" and "Code" links where they exist.
  - Mark Reema as live.
  - Replace the drawings with real screens from the three live demos.
- **Command:** /impeccable clarify

**2. [P1] The hero shows off the name, not the recruiter's facts**
- **Why it matters:** Recruiters come first. They look for role, stack, location, work permit, CV and email. All six are on the page, but they're small and scattered.
  - The role is an 11px label under a 116px name.
  - The work permit is the smallest text on screen. On a phone it falls below the first screen.
  - The CV is a small text link next to a filled button. It appears only once.
- **Fix:**
  - At 1440px, 55% of the hero is empty. Put a plain facts block there at 15-16px.
  - Include the role, Berlin plus EU or remote, and EU citizen with no sponsorship needed.
  - Add the stack, languages, the CV and the email.
  - Make "Download CV (PDF, 127 KB)" the filled button.
  - Add the CV link to the nav and the contact block.
- **Command:** /impeccable layout

**3. [P1] The look matches two AI-default looks the owner's rules ban**
- **Why it matters:** The rule says to avoid these looks unless the brief asks for one, and PRODUCT.md doesn't ask. A tech lead may think the page was generated. That's the wrong doubt for someone whose pitch is building products alone.
- **Fix:**
  - Choose a new direction before any polish.
  - Build the signature element from the edge. Real product screens would work. So would a strip on each card that shows the layers built.
  - Drop the italic accent words.
  - Replace the hairlines on every row with spacing and background panels.
  - Keep the accessibility and spacing work.
  - The redesign isn't committed, so changing course costs the least now.
- **Command:** /impeccable shape

**4. [P2] The biggest words speak to the wrong reader**
- **Why it matters:**
  - The section headings are puns, like "Built to work." and "Notes along the way."
  - The real section names sit in 11px labels, so screen readers and search engines get the puns.
  - The close, "Let's build something useful.", sounds like a freelancer pitching a client, but the site is for employers.
  - Screen readers also hear "Let's buildsomething useful", because the line break drops the space.
- **Fix:**
  - Make "Selected work", "Writing" and "Contact" the headings.
  - Rewrite the contact block for employers. Say the owner is open to senior full-stack and AI roles, add the work permit line, and link the CV.
  - Add a space before the <br /> in app/page.tsx:92.
- **Command:** /impeccable clarify

**5. [P2] Key labels are 10px caps, and one fails contrast on hover**
- **Why it matters:**
  - Recruiters scan the stack lines for keywords. At 10px caps they're hard to read.
  - Caps also change names, like "NEXT.JS" and "BOTO3". The detector flagged 13 of these labels.
  - On row hover, the group label drops to 4.40:1. That fails WCAG AA, which PRODUCT.md makes a firm rule.
- **Fix:**
  - Set stack and category labels in sentence case at 12-13px.
  - Keep caps for one label level only.
  - Merge the two near-identical greys into one that stays above 5:1 on the hover background. They measure 4.92:1 and 4.66:1 today.
- **Command:** /impeccable typeset
